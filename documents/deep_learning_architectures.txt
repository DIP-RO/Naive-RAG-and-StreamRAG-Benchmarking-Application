Deep Learning Architectures

Neural networks form the foundation of modern deep learning. Key architectures have evolved to handle different types of data and tasks.

Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) use convolutional layers to process grid-structured data like images. They apply filters that detect features such as edges, textures, and shapes. Pooling layers reduce spatial dimensions while preserving important information. CNNs power most computer vision systems including image classification, object detection, and facial recognition.

Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) process sequential data by maintaining a hidden state that captures information from previous steps. Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) variants address the vanishing gradient problem, enabling learning of long-range dependencies. These are widely used for time series forecasting and sequence modeling.

Transformer architectures introduced self-attention mechanisms that process all positions simultaneously rather than sequentially. The encoder-decoder structure with multi-head attention enables parallel computation and captures complex relationships regardless of distance. Transformers power Large Language Models like GPT-4, Claude, and LLaMA. Key innovations include positional encodings and layer normalization.

Diffusion models learn to reverse a gradual noising process, generating high-quality images from random noise. They have become the leading approach for image generation, powering systems like DALL-E, Stable Diffusion, and Midjourney.

Training large architectures requires significant computational resources. Techniques like gradient accumulation, mixed precision training, and distributed training across GPU clusters enable scaling to billions of parameters.
